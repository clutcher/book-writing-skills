# Marketing evidence, graded

What is known about book copy, listings and persuasion, with the source, the method and a grade
for every claim. **This file makes no recommendations.** It exists so any figure can be
rechecked, and so nobody runs a search that already came back empty. Gathered 28 August 2026;
store pages are in `store-limits.md`. Category measurements belong to each book, in its
`keywords-and-categories.md`.

## Grades

| Grade | Means |
|---|---|
| **STRONG** | Peer-reviewed with a behavioural measure, or a large A/B test with published numbers |
| **MODERATE** | Large study or industry report with a stated method, but correlational or self-reported |
| **WEAK** | Real data, but undisclosed method, wrong outcome measure, or vendor-run with a conflict |
| **OPINION** | No data. Recorded only where a claim is widely repeated and needs a counterweight |
| **VERIFIED-HERE** | Checked directly from the source in the session that recorded it |

**The provenance rule.** A research agent can invent rank values, prices and description
character counts and present them in the same voice as retrieved data. **Treat any figure resembling per-book rank, review velocity or description
length as absent unless it appears with a source.** When extending this file, add a row only
with a source, a method and a grade; write "not retrievable" rather than a result you could not
read.

## 1. Does description or back-cover copy affect sales?

**No published randomised test of description WORDING against book purchase exists.** Searched:
peer-reviewed marketing and library science, publisher A/B tests, KDP experiment results,
academic work on blurbs. A gap in the world, not in the search. What exists measures the
*presence* of descriptive data, not its quality:

| Finding | Source | Method | Grade |
|---|---|---|---|
| Titles with all four descriptive elements average 65% higher sales than titles with none; publishers newly supplying enhanced data saw +13% year on year | [Nielsen Book UK Metadata Study 2016](https://www.editeur.org/files/Events%20pdfs/Supply%20chain%202016/Nielsen%20Book%20UK%20Study%20The%20Importance%20of%20Metadata%20for%20Discoverability%20and%20Sales.pdf) | Top 100,000 UK titles, BookScan x bibliographic data | MODERATE |
| 3 descriptive elements: 72% higher sales vs none. Cover image alone: 51% higher | [Nielsen Book US Metadata Study 2016](https://www.editeur.org/files/Events%20pdfs/Supply%20chain%202016/Nielsen%20Book%20US%20Study%20The%20Importance%20of%20Metadata%20for%20Discoverability%20and%20Sales.pdf) | 97,397 records | MODERATE |
| Metadata-enhanced group +9% US, +22% UK | [Ingram metadata study](https://www.ingramcontent.com/publishers-blog/study-finds-improved-metadata-could-boost-sales-by-up-to-9) | 2,000 titles, two-group design | MODERATE, vendor conflict, description bundled with other changes |
| Blurb tests: genre awards +5%, three or more character names -10%, "accessible" -20% for business books | [BookBub blurb A/B tests](https://insights.bookbub.com/book-description-ab-tests-you-need-to-see/) | Randomised email split; outcome is clicks, not purchase; no sample sizes | WEAK |
| "Search Inside the Book" +9% sales | [Amazon press release, Oct 2003](https://press.aboutamazon.com/2003/10/amazon-com-announces-sales-impact-from-new-search-inside-the-book-feature) | Five-day window, non-random enrolment, never replicated | WEAK |

The Nielsen reports say so themselves: "we cannot measure direct causation, but just identify
the correlation." **Do not use** the "3x conversion from rewriting a description" claim that
circulates on author-service blogs: no method, no baseline. OPINION.

## 2. What drives a book purchase

| Finding | Source | Method | Grade |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Structure decides.** Table of contents, organisation and content structure dominated selection; **author credentials, publisher, date and authoritativeness did NOT affect it** | Stieve & Schoen (2006), *J. Academic Librarianship* 32(6) 599-608, [link](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0099133306001819) | Forced choice, 11 matched book pairs, 20 candidate factors, behavioural | **STRONG** |
| Author reputation, publisher reputation and cover attractiveness all significant; **author reputation significant only for technical books** | d'Astous, Colbert & Mbarek (2006), *Poetics* 34(2) 134-147, [link](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b62a4c55db0a23991bb1709d756be8fab07b469d) | Experimental manipulation of five variables; sample size not retrievable | STRONG design, incompletely retrieved |
| Most valued: title, synopsis, subject, recommendation, discount | Leitao et al. (2018), *J. Retailing & Consumer Services* 42, 88-97, DOI 10.1016/j.jretconser.2018.01.015 | Questionnaire, n=487, fiction buyers only | MODERATE |
| Purchase drivers: subject interest 29%, description 20%, series 16%, recommendation 16% | [BookNet Canada, Canadian Book Consumer 2024](https://www.booknetcanada.ca/canadian-book-consumer-2024) | n=4,212, self-report | MODERATE |

**Stieve & Schoen against d'Astous is a real tension**, not a resolved question. The plausible
reconciliation (reputation counts when already known; structure decides when it is not) is an
untested inference.

## 3. Reviews and social proof

| Finding | Source | Method | Grade |
|---|---|---|---|
| Purchase likelihood at 5 reviews is 270% higher than at 0; benefit falls off sharply after five; peaks at 4.0-4.7 stars | [Spiegel Research Center 2017](https://spiegel.medill.northwestern.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2021/04/Spiegel_Online-Review_eBook_Jun2017_FINAL.pdf) | ~13,500 products, 3 retailers, observational | MODERATE |
| Better reviews raise relative sales; 1-star reviews weigh more than 5-star | Chevalier & Mayzlin (2006), *JMR* 43(3) 345-354, [NBER](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w10148/w10148.pdf) | Differences-in-differences on book sales ranks, Amazon vs BN.com | **STRONG** |
| Review volume elasticity .236, valence .417 | You, Vadakkepatt & Joshi (2015), *J. Marketing*, [PDF](https://business.ucf.edu/wp-content/uploads/2015/04/meta-analysis-JM-published.pdf) | Meta-analysis, 51 studies | **STRONG** |
| Average review-sales correlation .091 | Babic Rosario et al. (2016), *JMR*, [link](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1509/jmr.14.0380) | Meta-analysis, 96 studies | **STRONG** |

Note the gap between the meta-analyses (.091) and the industry figures (270%). **No experiment
exists** on what replaces reviews for a zero-review launch; signalling theory (Kirmani & Rao
2000) is theory, not book evidence. Anyone quoting a number for it is guessing.

## 5. Description length

**No book-specific evidence exists.** Off-domain analogues only: longer Airbnb summaries *may*
raise occupancy ([arXiv:1711.09196](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1711.09196), WEAK); an inverted U
between product *title* length and sales in cross-border e-commerce
([PMC10635476](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10635476/), MODERATE, titles only).
"Longer converts better up to 1,000 words" traces to no study. OPINION.

## 6. Persuasion and copywriting research

There is a large, rigorous literature on what makes people CLICK and almost nothing on what
makes them BUY. Sort every claim by what was measured before using it.

### The fluency-truth effect is much weaker than its reputation

| Study | What it reports | Grade |
|---|---|---|
| Reber & Schwarz (1999), DOI [10.1006/ccog.1999.0386](https://doi.org/10.1006/ccog.1999.0386) | 8.36 vs 8.09 of 16; N=229 analysed; p<.05 one-tailed, d about 0.11; no replication found | WEAK |
| Oppenheimer (2006), DOI [10.1002/acp.1178](https://doi.org/10.1002/acp.1178) | Several stated p<.05 values fail two-tailed; Exp5 reverses with an obvious external cause | WEAK |
| Song & Schwarz (2008), *Psych Science*, DOI [10.1111/j.1467-9280.2008.02189.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2008.02189.x) | N=20, 27, 19; no replication found | WEAK |
| Alter & Oppenheimer (2009), DOI [10.1177/1088868309341564](https://doi.org/10.1177/1088868309341564) | A narrative review, not a meta-analysis, written before the replication failures | -- |
| **Meyer et al. (2015)**, DOI [10.1037/xge0000049](https://doi.org/10.1037/xge0000049) | 17 studies, >7,000 participants, 1.43 vs 1.42 of 3: "no evidence of a disfluent font benefit under any conditions" | **STRONG, definitive null** |
| Dechene et al. (2010), DOI [10.1177/1088868309352251](https://doi.org/10.1177/1088868309352251) | The **repetition**-based truth effect is robust; meta-analysis of 51 studies | **STRONG** |

**Use:** do not argue that clear copy is more *believed*. Argue that it is more *understood*,
which is separately evidenced.

### Reading level and plain language

| Finding | Grade |
|---|---|
| **The 95% / 98% lexical coverage thresholds are conventions with three strikes against them.** Hu & Nation 2000 never measured 98% (an extrapolation; its own claim puts any threshold between 80% and 90%); Schmitt et al. 2011 (N=661) found a linear relation and no threshold; the Kremmel et al. 2023 registered replication failed, DOI [10.1111/lang.12622](https://doi.org/10.1111/lang.12622) | STRONG (the failure) |
| Unbounce, 41,000+ pages: lower reading grade converts better; observational, no significance tests | WEAK |
| **No conversion test isolating reading level exists** | -- |
| Best causal plain-language evidence: Bhargava & Manoli (2015), *AER*, DOI [10.1257/aer.20121493](https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.20121493); randomised, N=35,050; bundled treatment | STRONG, bundled |
| NN/g lower-literacy study, N=50, double-blind: rewriting for lower-literacy readers raised their task success 46% -> 82% **and** higher-literacy readers' 68% -> 93% | MODERATE |

The assumed trade-off between serving A2 readers and serving experts is not visible in the one
study that measured both (small N, task success, not sales; sentences can still be too short).
**No peer-reviewed test of plain-language professional texts with second-language readers was
found** (the search covered one professional field); a claim that simplified English
measurably helps non-native readers of a book is transfer, not evidence. An A2 target rests on the author's decision about the audience, never on coverage
thresholds.

### Headlines

| Finding | Grade |
|---|---|
| **"Use *you* and conversions rise" is folklore.** Outbrain + HubSpot, 3.3 million paid headlines: you/your headlines did 36% worse on clicks and 33% worse on conversion (observational, confounded) | MODERATE, against the folklore |
| "Changing *your* to *my* lifted clicks 90%": the author's two write-ups describe different tests; no N | WEAK, misreported |
| Packard & Berger (2020), *Psych Science*, DOI [10.1177/0956797620902380](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797620902380): cited for "you works", but the authors argue the mechanism is NOT direct address | STRONG, often misapplied |
| Burnkrant & Unnava (1995), *JCR*, DOI [10.1086/209432](https://doi.org/10.1086/209432): self-reference persuades only when arguments are strong | STRONG, conditional |
| Questions vs statements: contradictory (Lai & Farbrot 2013 field experiments for questions; Backlinko 12.1M queries, no significant difference; a survey ranking questions last has no sample size) | mixed |
| Robertson et al. (2023), *Nature Human Behaviour*, DOI [10.1038/s41562-023-01538-4](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-023-01538-4): 22,743 tests, each extra negative word +2.3% clicks | STRONG, but clicks on news teasers |
| GoodUI, 17 headline tests: only 2 reach 80% power at a 5% effect; median power about 15% | MODERATE, the reality check for vendor case studies |

Of every practitioner headline case study retrieved, exactly one states a significance level
and none a sample size. **Folklore, origin untraceable:** "80% never get past the headline";
"traffic varies 500% by headline"; "number headlines beat everything"; "1 million headlines"
studies without method.

### What was actually measured

Purchases: four items in total, none clean. Clicks: essentially everything else. Shares:
selected on the outcome. Attitude: a few lab studies. **No copy decision on a book can be
justified by outcome evidence, because the evidence measures a different outcome.** Use the
lenses and judgement, and say so.

## 7. Conversion-optimisation evidence is largely illusory

Read this before citing any conversion claim.

- **Qubit, 6,700 e-commerce experiments (2017), method assured by PwC:** "90% of experiments have
  an effect of less than 1.2% on revenue, positive or negative"; colour changes +0.0% with a 49%
  chance of being positive (n=81); detecting a 1% uplift needs about 120,000 converters per
  variant. STRONG, own client base.
  [PDF via Wayback](https://web.archive.org/web/20170722062326if_/http://www.qubit.com/sites/default/files/pdf/qubit_meta_analysis.pdf)
- **Berman & Van den Bulte, "False Discovery in A/B Testing", *Management Science* (2022),**
  DOI [10.1287/mnsc.2021.4207](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2021.4207): about 70% of tested
  effects are true nulls; one in five results significant at 5% is ineffective in the field.
  STRONG.
- **Georgiev, 115 published A/B tests:** 70% underpowered; mean lift 12.33% falls to 3.77% after
  pruning. MODERATE-STRONG. His 1,001 tests: median lift 0.08%. MODERATE.
- **Goodson (Qubit, 2014):** stopping at p<.05 when checking daily gives a "significant" result
  on 41% of A/A tests. MODERATE (simulation).
- **Kohavi, *Trustworthy Online Controlled Experiments*:** only a third of tested ideas improve
  their metric at Microsoft, 10-20% in well-optimised domains. "Twyman's law" (anything that
  looks interesting is usually wrong) is itself of folklore attribution.
- **Folklore traced to origin:** "red beats green" (one test, ~2,000 visits, no p-value, killed
  by Qubit's colour row); "77% of A/A tests reach significance" (the simulation found 771/1000
  at 90% confidence, 531/1000 at 95%; cite that or nothing).

**Therefore: no copy decision can be justified by conversion evidence.** A cover cannot be A/B
tested at all. The defensible basis is the ten lenses, category conventions as a guard against
looking amateur, and, for a book whose subject has its own research, that research, fact-checked
in the book's sources. Where a marketing claim and fact-checked science disagree, the science
wins.

## 8. Searched, and not found

- Any randomised test of book description wording against purchase.
- Any published KDP experiment result for descriptions, with numbers.
- Any book-specific description-length data.
- Any eye-tracking study of a book's online product page.
- Any study of how readers in one profession choose books for their work (searched for one
  professional field; nearest: undergraduate library choice, and no published research by the
  field's own publishers).
- Any experiment on substitutes for reviews on a zero-review product.
- Nielsen "Books & Consumers" cover-versus-blurb percentages: paywalled.
