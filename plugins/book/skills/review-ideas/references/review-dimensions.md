# Review dimensions: what professional reviewers check

Research into what professional book reviewers, developmental editors, technical reviewers, copy editors, fact-checkers, and adversarial critics evaluate when reviewing a non-fiction book. Organized by reviewer perspective with granular check items.

**How the plugin uses this list.** The three stages of the review-ideas `editor` mode cover the developmental,
substantive, fact-checking, technical, adversarial, beta-reader and academic perspectives (use
this list to see what a stage may have skipped). Line editing, copy editing and proofreading
belong to the review-text skill; production standards to the build-editions skill; positioning
and market to the prepare-sales-text skill; legal and sensitivity questions go to a person. The
review-ideas `readers` mode gives one reviewer per perspective. This is a checklist to pick from, not a pass to run whole.

---

## 1. Developmental Editor

The developmental editor evaluates the manuscript at the highest level: whether the book works as a whole, whether its argument holds together, and whether it serves its audience.

### 1.1 Thesis and Central Argument

- Does a one-sentence core promise/thesis exist?
- Is the thesis stated clearly in the opening chapters?
- Do all chapters relate to and support the central thesis?
- Are there sections that stray off-topic from the thesis?
- Is the thesis novel, or does it rehash existing books?
- Does the book shed new light on a topic people care about?
- Can the reader identify the main idea within a few minutes?

### 1.2 Structure and Organization

- Is the chapter order logically sequenced (chronological, thematic, by complexity, deductive)?
- Are part/chapter/section distinctions clear and justified?
- Could chapters be reordered for stronger logical flow?
- Are there chapters that should be merged, split, added, or removed?
- Does the table of contents provide a lucid, engaging overview of the argument?
- Is the scope appropriate for the document length (too broad or too narrow)?
- Are long chapters divided into manageable sections with subheadings?
- Does the structure clarify meaning rather than obscure it?

### 1.3 Argument Quality and Evidence

- Is the argumentation well-developed and convincing?
- Are assumptions properly supported with evidence?
- Are there gaps in logic between claims?
- Are theories or concepts applied before they are introduced or explained?
- Are questions raised but never answered?
- Is every claim paired with proof and a demonstrating example?
- Are there too few or too many examples, case studies, and anecdotes?
- Are counterarguments addressed?
- Does the book rely on emotional appeal where logical argument is needed?
- Are the strongest objections to each claim acknowledged and engaged?
- Do conclusions follow from the evidence presented?

### 1.4 Coherence and Flow

- Does the content have cohesion and form a meaningful unit?
- Are transitions between ideas, paragraphs, sections, and chapters smooth?
- Are there rabbit trails unrelated to the topic?
- Do linking devices (through-lines, themes, keywords, circular events) connect the book?
- Is there too little or too much explanation for each idea?
- Are there dense sections that are difficult to assimilate?
- Does the narrative flow logically without requiring the reader to backtrack?

### 1.5 Pacing and Reader Engagement

- Does the opening grab and hook the reader?
- Does each chapter opening compel continued reading?
- Do chapter endings maintain intrigue or reinforce key points?
- Is the pace appropriate for the complexity of the information?
- Are there sections where pacing drags or feels rushed?
- Are scene/section lengths varied to maintain rhythm?
- Is there sufficient white space around key moments?
- Does the book generate the intended reader experience (insight, conviction, practical capability)?

### 1.6 Voice, Tone, and Authority

- Is the author's voice consistent throughout?
- Is the tone appropriate for the target audience?
- Does the authorial stance (expert, guide, or peer) remain consistent?
- Are there sections where authority level or formality shifts inappropriately?
- Does the voice convey the author's passion and expertise?
- Is the writing engaging rather than dry or clinical?

### 1.7 Audience Fit

- Has the target audience been clearly defined?
- Is the content appropriate for the audience's knowledge level?
- Is the language accessible without being condescending?
- Are key terms explained for the audience?
- Does the book meet genre conventions and reader expectations?
- Is the reading level appropriate for the target audience?

### 1.8 Completeness and Gaps

- Are important points glossed over that deserve fuller treatment?
- Are there gaps where additional examples, data, or explanation would help?
- Is the topic covered with reasonable completeness?
- What is omitted that might be important?
- Does the book address both theoretical and practical dimensions?
- Does the conclusion effectively summarize, restate the thesis, and tie ideas together?

### 1.9 Redundancy and Efficiency

- Are paragraphs or sections repetitive?
- Are there duplicate arguments or examples that serve the same purpose?
- Can wordy sections be streamlined without losing meaning?
- Is off-topic material identified and flagged for removal?
- Are there filler sections that do not advance the argument?

## 2. Substantive/Structural Editor

Operates between developmental and line editing. Focuses on how ideas are presented within chapters and sections.

### 2.1 Section-Level Architecture

- Does each section have a clear beginning, middle, and end?
- Does each section have a single clear purpose?
- Is the order of ideas within each section logical?
- Are sections appropriately sized (not too long, not too short)?
- Do subheadings accurately describe what follows?

### 2.2 Argument Presentation Within Sections

- Are claims presented before evidence, or evidence before claims, by deliberate design?
- Do case studies, figures, and sidebars land where they prove the point?
- Is there sufficient explanation for each idea (not too much, not too little)?
- Are abstract concepts grounded with concrete examples?
- Are practical implications drawn from theoretical points?

### 2.3 Paragraph-Level Logic

- Does each paragraph advance a single idea?
- Do paragraphs flow logically from one to the next?
- Are paragraph lengths varied and appropriate?
- Are transition words and phrases used effectively but not overused?
- Is there excess repetition of words, phrases, or sentence constructions?

### 2.4 Information Placement

- Is background/context delivered when the reader needs it (not too early, not too late)?
- Are definitions placed before terms are used?
- Are forward references and backward references clear?
- Are digressions justified and clearly marked?

### 2.5 Simplification of Complex Information

- Is language overly technical for the intended readers?
- Are complex ideas broken into simpler components with examples?
- Are analogies effective and accurate?
- Are technical terms defined on first use?
- Could visual aids (diagrams, tables, lists) replace dense prose?

---

## 3. Line Editor

Focuses on sentence-level craft: how each sentence reads, its clarity, rhythm, and precision.

### 3.1 Sentence Clarity and Precision

- Is each sentence clear on first reading?
- Are there ambiguous sentences that could be read multiple ways?
- Is word choice precise (the exact right word, not an approximation)?
- Are specialized terms used correctly and consistently?
- Are sentences free of jargon that excludes readers?

### 3.2 Sentence Structure and Variety

- Are sentence lengths varied (mix of short and long)?
- Are sentence structures varied (not all subject-verb-object)?
- Are there run-on sentences that should be broken up?
- Are there sentence fragments that need completion?
- Is passive voice used only when deliberately appropriate?

### 3.3 Concision and Wordiness

- Can sentences be tightened without losing meaning?
- Are there filler words (really, very, just, quite, somewhat)?
- Are there redundant phrases (past history, free gift, advance planning)?
- Are there wordy constructions that can be simplified?
- Are there cliches that should be replaced with fresh language?

### 3.4 Rhythm and Flow

- Do sentences read smoothly when read aloud?
- Are there clunky, verbose, or meandering sentences?
- Is the cadence of the prose appropriate for the content?
- Do transitional sentences bridge ideas effectively?

### 3.5 Showing vs. Telling

- Are claims demonstrated with evidence rather than merely asserted?
- Are examples vivid and specific rather than generic?
- Does the text show the reader why something matters rather than just stating it?

## 4. Copy Editor

Ensures mechanical correctness, consistency, and adherence to style standards.

### 4.1 Grammar and Syntax

- Subject-verb agreement throughout
- Correct verb tense usage and consistency
- Correct pronoun-antecedent agreement
- Pronoun reference clarity (no ambiguous "this" or "it")
- Correct use of who/whom, that/which, affect/effect
- Correct modifier placement (no dangling or squinting modifiers)
- Parallel construction in lists and compound structures
- Correct use of subjunctive mood where needed

### 4.2 Punctuation

- Correct comma usage (serial comma per style guide, introductory phrases, compound sentences)
- Correct semicolon and colon usage
- Correct dash usage (em-dash vs. en-dash vs. hyphen)
- Correct quotation mark usage (curly vs. straight, single vs. double)
- Correct apostrophe usage (possessives vs. contractions)
- Correct ellipsis formatting (with or without spaces per style guide)
- Consistent punctuation in bulleted/numbered lists
- End punctuation correct in all sentences

### 4.3 Spelling and Typographical

- All words spelled correctly
- Consistent spelling of proper names throughout
- Consistent spelling of technical terms throughout
- Correct use of capitalization
- Consistent hyphenation of compound words
- No doubled words or missing words
- Correct use of abbreviations and acronyms (spelled out at first use)
- Acronyms used only once flagged for removal

### 4.4 Style Guide Compliance

- Consistent application of chosen style manual (Chicago, AP, APA, MLA)
- Consistent number formatting (when to spell out vs. use numerals)
- Consistent date formatting
- Consistent treatment of titles (italics vs. quotes)
- Consistent use of Oxford comma (or not, per style guide)
- Consistent formatting of URLs, email addresses, phone numbers

### 4.5 Formatting Consistency

- Chapter title formatting consistent across all chapters
- Heading hierarchy consistent (H1, H2, H3 levels)
- Font type and size consistent throughout
- Italics and boldface usage consistent
- Bulleted and numbered list formatting consistent
- Block quote formatting consistent
- Code sample formatting consistent (for technical books)
- Table formatting consistent

### 4.6 Cross-Reference Accuracy

- Table of contents entries match chapter/section titles exactly
- Table of contents page numbers match actual pages
- Figure and table numbers are consecutive and correctly referenced in text
- Every cross-reference ("see Chapter 5") points to the correct location
- Index entries point to correct pages
- Footnote/endnote numbering is consecutive and correct
- Every citation in text has a matching bibliography entry
- Every bibliography entry is cited somewhere in the text

### 4.7 Continuity and Internal Consistency

- Names spelled consistently (people, places, concepts)
- Dates and timelines internally consistent (no contradictions)
- Numbers and statistics consistent when repeated
- Terminology used consistently (same term for same concept throughout)
- Facts stated in one place not contradicted elsewhere

## 5. Fact-Checker

Verifies accuracy of all factual claims in the manuscript.

### 5.1 Quotations

- Every direct quote is verbatim (word-for-word accurate)
- Quotes are attributed to the correct person
- Quotes are not taken out of context to misrepresent the source's meaning
- Page numbers for quotes are accurate
- Quotes from secondary sources are identified as such

### 5.2 Citations and References

- Every citation points to the correct source
- Authors, titles, dates, publishers, page numbers in bibliography are accurate
- Sources are primary (original) rather than secondary where possible
- Sources are reliable and credible
- Sources are current (not outdated for claims about current state)
- Every factual claim is traceable to a cited source

### 5.3 Names and Biographical Facts

- Names of people are spelled correctly
- Titles, positions, and affiliations are accurate
- Birth/death dates are correct
- Achievements and contributions are accurately attributed
- No confusion between people with similar names

### 5.4 Dates and Historical Claims

- All dates are verified against primary sources
- Historical events are accurately described
- Chronological sequences are correct
- "First" claims verified (first person to do X, first time Y happened)
- Historical context is not anachronistic

### 5.5 Numbers and Statistics

- Statistics are accurately reported from their sources
- Statistical methodology is not misrepresented
- Sample sizes and confidence levels are correctly stated
- Percentages add up correctly
- Currency amounts, measurements, and units are correct
- Numbers are consistent when the same fact appears in multiple places

### 5.6 Technical Accuracy (for Technical Books)

- Code examples compile and run correctly
- Code examples produce the stated output
- API names, method signatures, and library references are current and accurate
- Technical terminology is used correctly per industry standards
- Version numbers and release dates of tools/frameworks are accurate
- Technical claims about performance, behavior, or capability are verifiable
- Algorithms and data structures are correctly described
- Error messages and system behaviors match actual system behavior

### 5.7 Characterization of Sources

- Authors' positions are not misrepresented or oversimplified
- Nuanced arguments are not reduced to straw men
- Debates are presented fairly, not one-sidedly
- "Experts agree" claims are verified (do they actually agree?)
- Consensus claims distinguish actual consensus from majority opinion

## 6. Technical Reviewer (Specific to Technical Books)

Reviews the book for technical accuracy, practical relevance, and pedagogical effectiveness.

### 6.1 Technical Correctness

- Are all technical claims accurate?
- Are code examples syntactically correct and idiomatic?
- Do code examples follow current best practices for the language/framework?
- Are deprecated features, APIs, or patterns identified?
- Are version-specific claims clearly labeled with version numbers?
- Are performance claims substantiated with benchmarks or references?
- Are security implications of code examples addressed?

### 6.2 Completeness and Depth

- Does the book cover the topic with appropriate depth for the audience?
- Are important subtopics missing?
- Are edge cases and exceptions addressed?
- Are common mistakes and pitfalls covered?
- Are trade-offs discussed (not just "do this" but "why and when")?
- Is the difficulty progression appropriate (easy to hard)?

### 6.3 Practical Applicability

- Can readers apply the concepts in real-world projects?
- Are examples realistic (not toy examples that break down at scale)?
- Are the recommended approaches actually used in industry?
- Does the book distinguish between ideal and pragmatic approaches?
- Are there actionable takeaways from each chapter?

### 6.4 Pedagogical Effectiveness

- Are concepts introduced in the right order (prerequisites before advanced topics)?
- Does each chapter build on previous chapters appropriately?
- Are abstract concepts grounded with concrete examples?
- Are there enough examples to illustrate each concept?
- Do examples progress in complexity appropriately?
- Are summary sections helpful and accurate?
- Would a reader at the target skill level be able to follow the progression?

### 6.5 Currency and Relevance

- Is the technology stack current (not outdated)?
- Are recommended tools and libraries still maintained?
- Are industry trends accurately represented?
- Does the book acknowledge where the field is evolving?
- Are deprecated approaches marked as such?

## 7. Proofreader

The final quality gate before publication. Catches surface errors missed in earlier passes.

### 7.1 Text Accuracy

- No remaining spelling errors
- No remaining grammatical errors
- No remaining punctuation errors
- No doubled or missing words
- No transposed letters or words
- Correct word breaks and hyphenation at line ends

### 7.2 Page Layout and Typography

- Page numbers appear correctly and consistently
- Running heads match chapter/section titles
- Chapter titles match table of contents
- No widow lines (single line of a paragraph at top of page)
- No orphan lines (single line of a paragraph at bottom of page)
- No stacked hyphenation (multiple consecutive hyphenated line endings)
- Consistent page depth (text block height)
- Margins correct and consistent
- Correct typeface and size used throughout
- Consistent leading (line spacing)
- Text alignment consistent (justified, ragged right, etc.)

### 7.3 Front Matter

- Half-title page: title correct
- Title page: title, subtitle, author name correct
- Copyright page: copyright year, ISBN, publisher info, CIP data complete
- Dedication and epigraph present if planned
- Table of contents: accurate titles and page numbers
- List of figures/tables: accurate titles and page numbers
- Acknowledgments: names spelled correctly
- Foreword/preface: consistent with rest of book

### 7.4 Back Matter

- Bibliography formatting consistent per style manual
- Bibliography entries match in-text citations
- Glossary in alphabetical order with consistent formatting
- Index entries point to correct pages
- Index: proper alphabetization, cross-references work
- Appendix content correctly referenced from main text
- About the author: accurate and current

### 7.5 Figures, Tables, and Code

- Figure numbering consecutive and referenced in text
- Figure captions accurate and consistent in style
- Figures not reversed, cropped incorrectly, or too low resolution
- Table numbering consecutive and referenced in text
- Table column/row headings accurate
- Table data accurate and aligned
- Code examples formatted consistently
- Code indentation preserved correctly
- No line-break errors in code examples

## 8. Positioning and Market Reviewer

Evaluates the book's competitive positioning and market viability.

### 8.1 Market Analysis

- Is the target audience clearly defined and sufficiently large?
- Does the market need this book (gap in existing offerings)?
- How does this book differ from the 5-10 closest competitors?
- Is the title effective (clear, memorable, searchable)?
- Is the subtitle descriptive and keyword-rich?
- Does the book address a current need or timeless topic?

### 8.2 Competitive Differentiation

- What does this book offer that existing books do not?
- Is the unique value proposition clear and compelling?
- Are competitor weaknesses that this book addresses identified?
- Is the author's unique perspective or framework clearly articulated?
- Does the book have a memorable central concept or metaphor?

### 8.3 Author Credibility

- Does the author have relevant expertise and credentials?
- Is the author's experience reflected authentically in the writing?
- Does the author have a platform (audience, following, speaking history)?
- Are the author's qualifications stated early and naturally?

### 8.4 Book Packaging and Metadata

- Is the title discoverable (searchable keywords)?
- Does the cover design match genre expectations?
- Is the back cover copy compelling?
- Are endorsements from credible authorities present?
- Is the book description optimized for online retail?
- Are category/genre classifications accurate for discoverability?
- Does the book length match category norms?

## 9. Legal Reviewer

Reviews the manuscript for legal risks.

### 9.1 Copyright and Intellectual Property

- Is quoted material within fair use limits (quantity, nature, market effect)?
- Are permissions obtained for extended quotes, images, or other copyrighted material?
- Are all borrowed materials properly attributed?
- Are trademark names used correctly (proper capitalization, generic term alongside)?
- Does the title avoid trademarked terms without permission?
- Are song lyrics, poems, or other artistic works properly licensed?

### 9.2 Defamation and Privacy

- Are identifiable living persons characterized accurately and fairly?
- Could any content be interpreted as defamatory (false statements damaging reputation)?
- Are private facts about real people disclosed without consent?
- Are public figures discussed only regarding matters of public interest?
- Are case studies anonymized or permissions obtained?
- Is the boundary between opinion and fact clearly maintained?

### 9.3 Professional Liability

- Does the book disclaim liability for advice taken from its content?
- Are medical, legal, or financial claims appropriately qualified?
- Is the disclaimer adequate for the subject matter?
- Are recommendations presented as guidelines rather than guarantees?

---

## 10. Sensitivity/Authenticity Reviewer

Reviews for cultural accuracy, bias, and representational issues.

### 10.1 Representation

- Are all identity groups portrayed without stereotyping?
- Is cultural representation accurate and nuanced (not superficial)?
- Are examples and analogies free from cultural bias?
- Is gendered language avoided where not necessary?
- Are diverse perspectives represented in examples and references?

### 10.2 Language and Framing

- Is language free from terms with harmful connotations?
- Are historical events and figures discussed with appropriate context?
- Is technical terminology free from exclusionary or offensive origins?
- Are metaphors and analogies free from cultural insensitivity?

### 10.3 Accessibility of Perspective

- Does the book assume a particular cultural or economic context as default?
- Are global perspectives acknowledged where relevant?
- Is the writing accessible to non-native English speakers?

## 11. Adversarial/Red Team Reviewer

Systematically challenges the book's arguments, seeking weaknesses.

### 11.1 Steelman Critique (Strongest Objections)

- For each major claim, what is the strongest possible counterargument?
- Has the author addressed the best version of opposing views (not straw men)?
- Are there credible experts who would disagree, and are their views engaged?
- Would an informed skeptic find the arguments convincing?
- Are there assumptions the author takes for granted that a critic would challenge?

### 11.2 Logical Analysis

- Are there logical fallacies in the arguments?
  - Appeal to authority without evidence
  - False dichotomies (presenting only two options when more exist)
  - Slippery slope arguments without mechanism
  - Cherry-picking evidence (citing supporting evidence while ignoring contradictory)
  - Survivorship bias (citing successes without acknowledging failures)
  - Correlation presented as causation
  - Hasty generalization from insufficient examples
  - Circular reasoning (using the conclusion as a premise)
  - Moving the goalposts between chapters
  - Equivocation (using the same term with different meanings)
- Are conclusions proportional to the evidence (not overclaiming)?
- Are scope limitations acknowledged?

### 11.3 Completeness of Argument

- What does the book NOT address that it should?
- Are there important alternative frameworks or approaches dismissed too quickly?
- Does the book acknowledge the limits of its own recommendations?
- Are there contexts where the advice would fail, and are they mentioned?
- Does the book distinguish between universal principles and context-dependent advice?

### 11.4 Intellectual Honesty

- Does the author acknowledge uncertainty where it exists?
- Are trade-offs presented honestly (not just benefits of preferred approach)?
- Are sources cited fairly (not misrepresented or taken out of context)?
- Does the book engage with the strongest criticisms of its position?
- Are personal opinions clearly distinguished from established facts?
- Does the author avoid the claim of false consensus ("everyone agrees")?

### 11.5 Internal Consistency

- Do claims in later chapters contradict claims in earlier chapters?
- Are the same standards applied consistently (no special pleading)?
- Does the book follow its own advice?
- Are definitions stable throughout (no drift in meaning)?
- Are examples consistent with the principles they illustrate?

## 12. Beta Reader / Target Audience Reviewer

Simulates the actual reader experience.

### 12.1 First Impression and Engagement

- Were the first paragraphs and first page compelling?
- At what point (if any) did interest flag?
- Were there sections that felt like a chore to read?
- Was the overall reading experience enjoyable or laborious?
- Would you recommend this book to a colleague?

### 12.2 Comprehension

- Were there sections you had to reread to understand?
- Were there terms or concepts that were unclear?
- Did the examples help you understand the concepts?
- Could you explain the main thesis to someone else after reading?
- Were there prerequisite knowledge assumptions that were not met?

### 12.3 Practical Value

- Did you learn something new?
- Can you apply what you learned in your daily work?
- Were the actionable takeaways clear?
- Did the book change how you think about the topic?
- Were there enough concrete examples to guide application?

### 12.4 Emotional Response

- Did any sections generate strong reactions (agreement, disagreement, surprise)?
- Were there sections that felt condescending or preachy?
- Did the author's voice feel authentic and trustworthy?
- Were there moments of genuine insight or "aha" moments?

### 12.5 Pacing and Length

- Was the book the right length (too long, too short)?
- Were there chapters that could be cut without loss?
- Were there topics that deserved more coverage?
- Did the pacing match your reading stamina for this type of content?

## 13. Production/Publishing Standards Reviewer

Ensures the book meets professional publishing standards.

### 13.1 ISBN and Metadata

- ISBN assigned and correctly placed on copyright page
- ISBN matches print listing
- LCCN obtained (if targeting libraries)
- Metadata (title, subtitle, author, description) consistent across all platforms
- BISAC category codes correctly assigned
- Keywords/tags optimized for discoverability

### 13.2 Copyright and Legal Pages

- Copyright notice with correct year
- Rights reservation statement
- Edition number (if applicable)
- Print history (if applicable)
- ISBN for each format (print, ebook, audiobook)
- Publisher contact information
- Permissions acknowledgments for borrowed material
- Disclaimer (if applicable)

### 13.3 Physical Book Specifications

- Trim size appropriate for genre (e.g., 6x9 for technical nonfiction)
- Paper weight and type appropriate
- Binding type appropriate for length and use
- Spine text correct and readable
- Cover finish appropriate (matte vs. glossy)
- Bleed settings correct for images that extend to page edge

### 13.4 Digital Formats

- EPUB validates without errors
- Table of contents navigable in e-reader
- Images display correctly at various screen sizes
- Hyperlinks functional
- Font embedding correct
- Reflowable text works at different font sizes

---

## 14. Academic/Scholarly Peer Reviewer

Evaluates the work against academic standards (relevant for books making scholarly claims).

### 14.1 Originality and Contribution

- Does the work make a novel contribution to the field?
- Is the contribution clearly articulated?
- Does it advance understanding beyond existing literature?
- Is the synthesis original even if individual sources are known?

### 14.2 Literature Review

- Is the existing literature adequately surveyed?
- Are the most important and relevant sources cited?
- Are recent developments in the field acknowledged?
- Is the relationship between this work and prior work clear?
- Are omissions from the literature review justified or accidental?

### 14.3 Methodology

- Is the approach clearly described and justified?
- Is the reasoning transparent and reproducible?
- Are limitations of the approach acknowledged?
- Are alternative approaches considered and reasons for rejection given?

### 14.4 Evidence Quality

- Is evidence drawn from credible, peer-reviewed sources?
- Is anecdotal evidence distinguished from empirical evidence?
- Are sample sizes and study designs adequate for the claims made?
- Is the evidence current (not relying on superseded research)?

## Summary: Cross-Cutting Dimensions

These dimensions recur across multiple reviewer types:

| Dimension | Reviewer Types That Check It |
|-----------|------------------------------|
| Accuracy of facts and claims | Fact-checker, Technical, Academic, Copy editor |
| Logical coherence of argument | Developmental, Substantive, Adversarial, Academic |
| Consistency (terms, facts, style) | Copy editor, Proofreader, Developmental, Substantive |
| Audience appropriateness | Developmental, Beta reader, Market, Sensitivity |
| Evidence quality and sufficiency | Developmental, Fact-checker, Academic, Adversarial |
| Clarity of prose | Line editor, Copy editor, Beta reader, Substantive |
| Cross-reference accuracy | Copy editor, Proofreader, Production |
| Legal compliance | Legal reviewer, Copy editor (permissions), Production |
| Competitive positioning | Market reviewer, Developmental (market fit) |
| Completeness of coverage | Developmental, Technical, Academic, Adversarial |
