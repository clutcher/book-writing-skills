# Building and showing options

Every option is a **pruned fix that can be dropped straight into the text, shown in full context,
with an explicit fit check**. These rules stop the common failures: options shown as pieces of
sentences, options that ignore the paragraph, and options that are versions of a wrong frame.

## Rule 0: prune before you show

Only options that pass the framing check reach the author. Do not show a fix built on a frame you
doubt. Do not add a closing "deeper analysis" that argues against your own first option: move
that reasoning into the framing check, and let it delete the wrong options. Fewer correct options
are better than many variants.

## Rule 1: full sentence, never a piece

Quote the complete new sentence(s), with **bold** on the changed words only. Never
`"...rose overnight in the cold."` A piece hides whether the sentence still reads, still links
to the one before, and still prepares the one after. Even a three-word change is shown as the
whole sentence.

## Rule 2: show the change in its context

The context grows with how far the change reaches:

- **Surface, a swap inside one sentence**: the full sentence, bold on the change.
- **Flow, a change inside a paragraph**: the new sentence **with its neighbours** (before, new,
  after), so both joins are visible.
- **Split, merge, insert, delete, reorder** (the number of sentences changes): **the whole
  paragraph** as it will read after the edit, bold on what changed. A split that reads well
  alone can leave the last sentence stranded or break the link to the next paragraph; only the
  whole paragraph shows this.

## Rule 3: the smallest edit first; removal is a real option

The smallest change that fixes the real cause is usually chosen. Sentences that only announce,
point forward or add nothing are usually deleted, not reworded. Always offer the minimal edit as
a real option. When the cause is `b` (announcement) or `e` (repetition), offer **removal**.

- Do not add sentences to repair an announcement sentence. Delete the announcement.
- When a paragraph-length rule of the book is at stake (too few or too many sentences), first try
  moving a paragraph break. Do not write new sentences just to reach a count.
- A minimal change stays minimal: do not fold other improvements into it unless the author asks.

## Rule 4: a fit check on every option (seven points)

After you put the option in place, test it against the Idea map. Each option ends with one line
that confirms, or names the failure of, all seven:

1. **One idea**: the paragraph still carries its one idea; no second idea slipped in.
2. **Chain and cause**: links and reasoning still hold; the cause is right (not a "because" that
   only repeats); no sentence now repeats or jumps.
3. **Topic, development, conclusion**: the topic still frames, the development still moves, the
   conclusion still sums up or leads on.
4. **Both joins**: the edit still links from the sentence or paragraph before and into the one
   after (read before, edited, after, across a heading too).
5. **Example and domain**: the edit keeps the chapter's running example and its domain; do not
   bring in a new cast of example names.
6. **The book's reasons stay the reasons**: when the book names a central cause, that cause is
   still the reason; the paragraph keeps one point of view; a means stays a means and is not
   turned into the goal.
7. **The book's word**: the wording uses the book's own term for the concept and does not drift
   from how this chapter and earlier chapters use it. Check the glossary and the coined terms.

An option that fails a point is shown with the failure named ("clean sentence, but the next
paragraph's back-reference now points at nothing") or dropped. Never show an option whose cost
to the paragraph is not stated.

## Rule 5: bring in no new problem

The fix must not trade its defect for another: no new side remark between dashes, no new unclear
"this" (above all right after a heading), no term used before it is introduced, no harder word,
no hedge or filler, no break in term use. Say when an option is dropped for this reason.

## Rule 6: rank lightly, with reasons

Give two to four options that survive, and a short order with reasons based on the fit check and
the cause, not on taste. The ranking is advice; the author often picks another. Never argue
against your own ranking later in the same note. Point out effects on linked notes.

## The shape of one option

```
- Option 1 (minimal): "{sentence before, plain} **{new sentence, changes in bold}** {sentence after, plain}"
  Fit: one idea kept; "therefore" still links the claim before; conclusion still leads into
  the next section; example unchanged; the book's reasons still drive it; the word ("rise") is the
  one the chapter used two sections earlier; both joins hold.
```

## Showing the diff

When the author asks to see a change before it is applied, show before and after, with the
changed words in bold in the after version. Say which words are a quote from a source and which
are the book's own words, and say whether the claim was checked against its source.
